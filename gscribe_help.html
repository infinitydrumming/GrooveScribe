Help!

Keyboard shortcuts:
  • Spacebar - Start/Stop midi playback
  • Ctrl-Z - Undo
  • Ctrl-Y - Redo
  • Left Arrow - Tempo Down
  • Right Arrow - Tempo Up
  • CTRL and mouse over - turn on notes
  • ALT and mouse over - turn off notes
  • Shift + drag over the notes (or ⌘ + drag on a Mac) - select some notes to copy
  • Ctrl-C, Ctrl-V (⌘-C, ⌘-V on a Mac) - copy the selected notes, and paste them where you Shift-click
  • Esc - let go of the selected notes

Saving a groove

Your groove is saved in its link: open the link any time and the groove comes back exactly as it was. Press Save & Share (top right) to:
  • Copy link - paste it into a message, a note or your bookmarks.
  • Save to My Grooves - keeps it on this device. Find it again under Grooves › My Grooves (the × removes it). My Grooves lives in this browser only, so keep the link too if you change phone or clear your browsing data.
  • WhatsApp or Email - send the link to yourself, a student or your teacher.
  • Print / PDF and MIDI file.

Sending an exercise or assignment

A link can carry a whole exercise, not just the notes. Set it up, then open Save & Share:
  • Include practice settings (ticked to start with) puts everything you've set up for practising into the link: the alphabet and its ticked figures with "Play each bar", the auto speed-up (how much, every so many minutes or bars, "Stop at"), count-in, the click's Solo and offset start, the practice timer, groove / click bars and muted lines. Untick it to send just the groove (with its tempo, swing and click).
  • Instructions: write what to do ("Play each figure 4 times, then speed up..."). They show at the top of the page when the link is opened, and print with the music. The × hides them.
Whoever opens the link can still change anything: the link sets things up, it doesn't lock them. Exercises of up to 32 bars fit in a link, settings and all.

Title, author and comment

The groove's title, author and comment show at the top of the page and on the sheet music. Click the title (or the pencil next to it) to change them, and to show the key to the drum symbols.

Student view

Press Student view (top right of the toolbar) to hide the editing tools and keep just the music, the counting and the play bar. Press Edit to change the groove again. The view is saved in the link, so you can send a student a groove that opens in the student view.

Tempo

Drag the tempo slider in the play bar, or tap the BPM number and type a tempo (30 to 300), then press Enter (Done on a phone). Change it while the groove plays and you hear the new tempo from the next bar line, so you can slow the alphabet down for a hard section, or speed it up, without stopping. The cursor and the music keep their place.

Sections (A, B...) and how many times each plays

Next to each bar's number in the grid is a Section chip. Choose a letter for the bars of a section: bars in a row with the same letter are one section (for example A for 2 bars of paradiddles, B for 2 bars of doubles). On a section's first bar a second chip says how many times it plays (×1 to ×16).
  • The groove plays each section that many times, then the next (A ×4, then B ×2), and round again. Bars without a letter play once, where they are. The practice timer, speed-up and click options count every bar that plays.
  • The music writes each section once, with its letter in bold in a box over it (a rehearsal mark, as in a score), repeat signs round it and how many times (×4) under the end repeat sign, and numbers the bars: a number starting a line sits by the clef, just over the stave.
  • Form: once there are sections, a Form box appears above the bars. Type the order the sections play, for example ABAC, or ABCA ABCA (a space between groups, to read it easily). Each letter plays its section as many times as its chip says; leave the box empty to play the sections in order. The music still writes each section once, with Form: A B A C under the title, like a chart.
  • Type a section's letter in Practise bars (B, or A, B) to drill just that section.
  • Sections and the form are saved in the groove's link, and stay with their bars when bars are added, copied or removed.

Practising some bars

To learn a groove or a section a bit at a time, type the bars to play in Practise bars in the play bar (Bars on a phone), then press Enter: one bar (3), a run of bars (1-2) or a list (2, 5 or 2 5). Only those bars play, round and round, and the music shows just them, headed "Bars 1-2" and numbered as in the whole groove (the other bars are greyed out in the grid, where you can still change them). Add the next bar as you get it (1-2, then 1-3, then 1-4...), and empty the box to play and show everything again. With sections, a letter practises a section (B). The practice timer, speed-up and groove / click bars count just the bars that play. Changed while playing, the new bars start when the ones playing come round to the end. The box is there for a groove of 2 or more bars, and with the alphabet on it is the alphabet's (the same box, in the same place). The choice is saved in the link with the other practice settings.

Metronome

Choose the click from the Click menu (next to the metronome icon). Every bar starts with a different sound on the 1.
  • bar - one click per bar, on the 1.
  • 4ths - adds a cowbell on the 1 and a click on the 2, 3, and 4;
  • 8ths - Adds a click on all the "ands" (plays 8th note triplets in triplet subdivisions)
  • 16ths - Adds a softer click on every 16th note. Plays a louder click on the 2, 3, and 4. (plays 16th note triplets in triplet subdivisions)
  • triplets - 3 clicks a beat, sextuplets - 6 a beat (the triplets louder), whatever notes the groove is in.
  • swung 8ths - a click on the beat and on the last triplet ("1 . a"), for playing over triplet and shuffle grooves; swung 16ths - the same at double speed.
  • dotted 4ths - a click every dotted quarter note, the pulse of 6/8 and 12/8.
  • A beat is the bottom of the time signature: in 7/8 the triplets click three to every 8th note.
The ... button next to the Click menu has the click's other options:
  • Solo - Plays the metronome, and mutes the groove when the play button is pressed.
  • Auto Speed Up - Will increase the metronome BPM automatically after each loop through. Choose whether it speeds up every so many minutes or every so many bars, for example 5 bpm every 16 bars. Tick "Stop at" and enter a tempo to keep increasing until that target tempo, then hold it.
  • Practice timer - Plays for a set number of minutes or bars, then stops by itself: the groove finishes the bar it's in and ends on a crash. The player shows what's left ("4:12 left", "12 bars left"). The timer is saved in the groove's link, so you can send a student "play this for 10 minutes". Click it again in the menu to turn it off.
  • Music spacing - How wide bars of music are drawn, grooves and the alphabet alike: move the slider left for narrower bars, more on a line (down to a quarter as wide: four times as many, drawn smaller if their notes need it, to look over a piece or for a big screen or TV), right for wider ones, fewer on a line, as far as one bar a line. In the middle (100%) the music is laid out as usual. Each device remembers its setting, so each student's can suit them.
  • Cursor timing - If the blue playback cursor runs ahead of (or behind) the sound on your device, for example over Bluetooth, move the slider until it lines up. Each device remembers its setting.
  • Flams, drags & ruffs - How far apart the grace notes are, and how loud. Spread them right out to practise slowly, then bring them back in. By default a flam's grace note is 30 ms before its note, and drags and ruffs are a little more open, 45 ms between each note. The setting is saved in the groove's link, so you can send a student a slowed-down version.
  • Offset click - Allows the metronome to accent the "e", "&", or "a", instead of the 1.

Counting

Pick a count in the Count menu (next to Click) to write it over the notes. Every note counts each note of the note setting: "1 e & a" in 16ths, "1 &" in 8ths, "1 & a" in triplets, "1 a li & a la" in 16th triplets (short for "1 ah lee & ah la"), "1 a li bi & a la ba" in 32nds (short for "1 ah lee bee & ah lah bah", in smaller letters so a bar fits on a phone), and "1 2 3" in 8th-note bars like 3/8 and 5/8 (8th-note triplets in 6/8: "1 a & 3 a &"). With notes beat by beat, each beat is counted its own way. A beat with stickings (R / L) keeps its stickings. Rhythm in bold counts every note too, with the rhythm in bold and the rest small and grey: the kick, snare and toms (not their ghost notes) and any accent are the rhythm; the hi-hat, crash and ride lines and the hi-hat foot are the ostinato. In the alphabet only its line (kick, snare or both) is in bold. Bold with ghosts is the same with ghost notes in bold too. Rhythm only counts just where something plays (every line), and always the beat numbers, in brackets when nothing plays on them: "1 e & (2) & a". The click counts what the click plays, in its own subdivision ("1 & a" for triplets, "1 a li & a la" for sextuplets, "1 a" for swung 8ths, the beats when the click is off), and follows it when you change the click; it replaces the stickings. Count: off takes the counting off. The count is saved in the groove's link.

A time signature for each bar

Each bar has a small time signature button (e.g. 4/4) under its swing / straight switch. Click it to give that bar its own time signature, for example a bar of 2/4 in a 4/4 song, or the 3/8 and 5/8 bars of "Here Comes the Sun".
  • The sheet music shows the new time signature wherever it changes, and the playback, metronome, cursor and groove / click bars follow each bar's length.
  • The bar keeps its notes from the start: a shorter time signature cuts the end off, a longer one adds rests.
  • The time signature button at the left of the toolbar sets every bar at once.
  • New bars copy the last bar's time signature.
  • Bars in x/8 are beamed the classic way, in 8th and 16th notes alike: 3/8 as one group of three, 5/8 as 3+2, 7/8 as 2+2+3 (6/8, 9/8 and 12/8 stay in threes). The grid is spaced in the same groups.
  • A note setting has to fit every bar, and the alphabet is only for grooves whose bars all have the same time signature (see The Alphabet for which).
  • Triplets work in x/4 and x/8 bars. In x/8, 16th-note triplets put three notes on every 8th note (like 8th-note triplets in 4/4), and 8th-note triplets put three over every two 8th notes (like quarter-note triplets), so they need bars of whole quarter notes such as 6/8 and 12/8, not 7/8.

Notes beat by beat (quarter-note triplets, fills...)

Over each bar is a row of BEATS. Tap a beat to choose its notes: 8ths, 16ths, 32nds, 8th triplets, 16th triplets, quarter-note triplets (over that beat and the next) or 32nd-note triplets. For example, write a 16ths groove, then tap beat 3 of the last bar and choose quarter-note triplets for a fill, or beat 4 and choose 32nds for a quick fill into the next bar.
  • The groove changes to the Mixed note setting. Each beat's label says its notes, and the grid shows only the notes each beat uses, so there is nothing extra to click.
  • The music, the sound, the cursor and the counting follow each beat.
  • In x/8 bars a beat is an 8th note: one 8th, two 16ths, a 16th-note triplet, or 8th-note triplets over that beat and the next.
  • When every beat is the same again, the groove goes back to that note setting (all 16ths: Notes 16ths).
  • 32nds and triplets can't be in the same groove: with a beat of 32nds, the triplet choices are greyed out (and the other way round). 8ths and 16ths go with either. The alphabet isn't for a groove with a beat of 32nds.
  • Notes on places a beat no longer uses are taken off. Undo brings them back.
  • Copying and pasting a bar, adding a bar and changing a bar's time signature keep its beats. Each bar's beats are saved in the groove's link.

Tom ghost notes, accents and rim clicks

Right click (or press and hold) a tom note and choose Tom Accent, Ghost Note or Rim Click. A rim click is written as an x on that tom's line and plays the cross-stick sound; the grid shows an x in the tom's circle.

Flams, drags and ruffs

Right click a note on the snare, the kick, a tom or a cymbal line (hi-hat, crash or ride) and choose Flam (one grace note), Drag (two) or Ruff (three). The note shows a small label (fl, dr or ru) and the grace notes are written on the sheet music in front of it.
  • Grace notes on… plays and writes the grace notes on a different drum, for example a flam from the snare into the high tom, or a drag on the snare into the kick.
  • The grace notes are timed in milliseconds just before their note, whatever the tempo, and never move the notes around them, so you can play exactly what is written at any speed.
  • Use Metronome Options > Flams, drags & ruffs to slow the grace notes down for practice.
  • No flam / drag / ruff takes it off again; turning the note off does too.

Diddles (double strokes)

Right click a note on any drum or cymbal and choose Diddle (double stroke): the note plays as two strokes, each half as long as the note setting's notes. A 16th-note diddle is two 32nds, an 8th-note diddle two 16ths, an 8th-triplet diddle two 16th triplets.
  • The music shows one slash through the note's stem (the standard sign for "twice as fast"), and the grid a red slash on the note.
  • An accented note's second stroke is played unaccented. A diddle can go with a flam, drag or ruff.
  • A note that shares its stem with another (a snare under the hi-hat) has the slash on the shared stem; only the drum with the diddle plays twice.
  • Choose it again to take it off; turning the note off does too.

What swing is "correct" or "normal"?

There is no "correct" value for swing. The level of swing varies widely and generally left to the performer to determine.
The classic triplet swing feel shows up at 33% on the swing meter. For a tighter swing feel (dotted 8th followed by 16th) move the meter to 50%, full swing. Swing is a matter of personal taste so please feel free to practice your grooves with different amounts of swing until you find the feel that you are looking for.
Wikipedia has a good article on the types of swing. (Scroll down to the "Types" header)

Groove Scribe    
Swing		  
20% 3:2 ratio     light
33% Triplet quarter + triplet eighth       2:1 ratio medium
50% Dotted eighth + sixteenth 3:1 ratio heavy

Swing works on 8th and 16th notes. With the Mixed note setting (notes beat by beat), the beats of 8ths and 16ths swing and the triplet beats play as written. Triplet note settings can't be swung (the slider shows "N/A").

Straight and swung bars

Under each bar's copy and paste buttons there is a small swing switch. Click it to make that bar straight: it then plays straight even though the groove is swung (with the swing slider's amount and style, regular or Brazilian, in the other bars). Click again to swing it. When a swung groove has straight bars, the sheet music shows "Swing" or "Straight" over the first bar and over every bar where the feel changes. The setting is saved in the groove's link.

Brazilian swing

Click the word SWING in front of the swing slider to switch it to BRAZIL, and click again to switch back. Brazilian swing (as in samba and partido alto) works on all four 16th notes of the beat: the "e" is played a little late and the "a" a little early, so the beat feels long-short-short-long: 1 - e & - a. The slider sets how far the "e" and the "a" move. At 10% the beat is split about 27.5% / 22.5% / 22.5% / 27.5%, a typical samba feel; 15% gives about 29% / 21%, and higher settings exaggerate it. In grooves written in 8th notes the same feel is spread over each pair of beats. The setting is saved in the groove's link. The sheet music stays straight, as Brazilian music is normally written.

The Alphabet (permutations)

The Alphabet is our name for permutations: a method for playing all the possible combinations of a given instrument for a note division. Choose one in the Alphabet menu (the ABC button): the kick, the snare, or kick and snare alternating (kick lead or snare lead), in the groove's note setting. Play each bar plays every figure that many times (up to 16) before the next one.

Rep counter: tick it (next to Play each bar) to see, just above the play bar while the alphabet plays, which bar of the music it's on and which time through it: "Bar 7 · Rep 2 of 4" (with an ostinato of more than one bar, which bar of that too: "· ostinato 2/3"). The device remembers the switch.

Time signatures: the alphabet works in 4/4 in every note setting, and in other time signatures where the figures fit the beats (the Alphabet button is dimmed where they don't):

  • x/4 (2/4, 3/4, 5/4, 7/4...): in 1/16 notes, 1/32 notes and both triplet settings, the 4/4 figures beat by beat. In 1/8 notes the figures last two beats, so only with an even number of beats (2/4, 6/4...).
  • 6/8, 9/8, 12/8 (and 3/8, 15/8), in dotted-quarter beats: in 1/8 notes they have the 1/8 triplet figures (3 eighths a beat, named by the count: "Singles on 2"), in 1/16 notes the 1/16 triplet figures (6 sixteenths a beat).
  • The cross-rhythms (every 2nd or every 4th note) are there when they come round evenly in the bar, so not in 9/8 8ths or 3/4 8th triplets, for example.
  • Not in 5/8, 7/8 and other bars of uneven beats, x/16, or grooves whose bars have different time signatures.
  • Changing to another time signature with the same figures (4/4 to 3/4 in 16ths, say) keeps the ticked figures.

An ostinato of more than one bar: the groove's first bars, up to 5, are the alphabet's ostinato (bars after the 5th are closed when an alphabet is chosen; Undo brings them back). 5 bars of 16ths leave room for a 5-note grouping to come round over the bar lines. Every figure is played over each bar of it, and Play each bar counts the times through the ostinato: ×2 with a 2-bar ostinato gives each figure 4 bars. The music writes each figure once over the ostinato's bars, and every bar of a figure has the figure's number, the number to type in Practise bars. Each bar keeps its own swing / straight switch and muted lines, and fat back follows each bar's snare. To add more bars, turn the alphabet off.

Fat back: tick it in the kick alphabet's options and every figure leaves the backbeat to the snare: there is no kick wherever the groove's snare plays (normally 2 and 4), only the kicks around it. Ghost notes don't count, so kicks under them stay. The headings over the figures say "fat back", and it is saved in the link with the other practice settings.

Simplify multiple kicks (the kick alphabet in 1/16 notes in 4/4): the kick on the "1" and the "1 e" is skipped when it directly follows a kick on the "4 a" of the bar before, so the figures flow when the whole run is played through. Use Accent Grid (the snare alphabet) accents the figure and plays ghost notes on the other notes of the grid.

Practising some bars: the bars of the alphabet are numbered in the music. Type the ones to practise in Practise bars in the play bar (Bars on a phone): one bar (5), a run of bars (3-6) or a list (2, 5, 9-10). Only those bars play (each "Play each bar" times), and the music shows just them with their numbers, every bar as wide and no wider than reads easily (two a line on a laptop, one on a phone; Options → Music spacing makes them narrower or wider on your device, as it does all the music). Empty the box to play the whole run again. The choice is saved in the link with the other practice settings.

Practising bars in an order: the same box plays the bars in the order you type them, and as many times as you like: 1 2 1 3; 1x4 2x2 1x4 3 (bar 1 four times, bar 2 twice...; without an x a bar plays "Play each bar" times); 1-4x2 (each of bars 1 to 4 twice); 1-4 1-4 7 (bars 1 to 4 twice through, then bar 7); 6-3 (6, 5, 4, 3). The music is written out as it plays, so you can always read ahead: each bar in turn, between repeat signs with how many times under them (×4), with the order over it all (Order: 1×4 2×2 1×4 3); the rep counter follows it.

Trade: trade bars are grooves or fills played between the figures, for time-and-fill practice ("3 bars of time, then the figure"). Choose how many trade bars (1 to 4) next to Trade in the Alphabet Options: they appear in the grid after the ostinato's bars, marked Trade, with every line showing. They start as copies of the groove's first bar: write any groove or fill in them, or paste a bar copied from another tab. Then choose how many times they play (×1 to ×8) and whether before or after each bar of the run, so "1 bar, ×3, before each bar" is 3 bars of time, then the figure. The music is written out as it plays, so it reads straight down without jumping back: the trade bars before (or after) each bar, marked Trade, between repeat signs with ×3 under them; the figures keep their numbers. The rep counter shows "Trade · 2 of 3" while they play. The "+" under the bars adds another trade bar; removing them all turns trading off. Everything is saved in the link.

1/16 triplets (and 16ths in 6/8, 9/8, 12/8): the alphabet has every rhythm of a beat of 6 notes, in a list that builds up a hit at a time. First the figures over a whole beat, each starting on any of the 6 notes: Singles; Single, gap, single; Doubles; Double, gap, single; Double, 2 gaps, single (started on note 3 or 5 it is single, gap, double); Triples; Triple, gap, single; Quads; Fives; All six. Then the ones that repeat inside the beat: Singles and Doubles twice a beat, every 2nd note, and the every-4th-note cross-rhythm. The whole-beat figures start unticked: tick a group, or single figures, to add them.

Editing tips

  • The "Hi-hat", "Snare", "Kick" and "Sticking" labels to the left of the editing grid have a menu when clicked. You can quickly turn all the notes in a measure on or off using the menu.
  • Right click a note to choose its articulation: accent, ghost note, open hi-hat, cross stick, buzz stroke, flam, drag, ruff, diddle and so on. On a phone or tablet, press and hold the note, or turn on the "Articulations" button: while it is on, tapping a note opens its articulations instead of turning it on or off. (On iPhones and iPads use the button: they don't always pass a press and hold on.)
  • Use the Grooves menu to select a groove close to the one you want, or use it to clear the editing grid to start a new groove from scratch.
  • Tap a menu's button again (Grooves, Help, the "ABC" Alphabet button, Save & Share...) to close it.
  • Add a copy, under each bar, puts the same bar again right after it, wherever it is in the groove: its notes, time signature, swing / straight switch and muted lines. The bars after it move along one. Undo takes it back.
  • The copy and paste buttons at the top of each bar copy a bar into another bar, or into another groove: in another tab or window, in the home-screen app, or on either of the site's addresses. The bar goes by way of your device's clipboard, so the first paste may ask to see the clipboard: allow it (on an iPhone or iPad, tap "Paste" in the bubble).
  • Copying some notes: hold Shift (or ⌘ on a Mac) and drag a box over the notes you want, on any lines, in one bar or across bars; they light up and a bar with Copy, Paste and Done appears (or use Ctrl-C). Shift-click the note where the copy should start, in any bar, and paste (Ctrl-V). The copy keeps its own lines (copied snare notes go on the snare line, wherever you click) and replaces what's there on them, rests included, with their accents, flams and diddles; other lines stay as they are. Undo takes a paste back. On a phone or tablet, turn on Select (next to Articulations): drag a box with your finger, tap Copy, tap the note where it should start, then tap Paste; tap Done to play notes again. Copied notes paste into another tab or window too (and on the site's other address), by way of the clipboard like a copied bar. Pasted into another note setting they keep their time (16ths into 32nds or Mixed); between triplets and straight notes they can't be pasted.
  • The eraser at the top of each bar takes all its notes off (flams and diddles too), so you can write just that bar again; its time signature, beats and section stay. Undo takes it back. (Clear all, at the bottom, gives a choice: Clear the notes empties every bar and keeps the rest; Clear everything also takes away the sections, the form, the bars to practise and the alphabet's order and trade bars, to start a new form and structure from scratch. Undo takes either back.)
  • Changing the Notes: keep the phrase or the timing. The switch after the Notes buttons (on a phone: at the foot of the Notes menu) says what a change of Notes does to the notes already written:
    • Keep: phrase (to start with): the notes keep the order they come in, slot after slot, across the bars, as if the phrase were played through the new setting (as a new time signature does). A paradiddle written as 16ths is played as 8ths, as 16ths again, or as triplets. A 3/4 groove of 16ths (12 notes a bar) changed to 4/4 and then to 8th triplets (also 12 a bar) has each of its bars back as it was, in triplets. The bars stay as many: notes past the end are cut, rests fill what's left. Changing back undoes it, and so does Undo.
    • Keep: timing: the notes stay where they are in the beat, so a groove of 16ths changed to 32nds is the same groove with room for 32nd-note fills (and 8ths keep the notes that fall on 8ths).
    Between triplets and straight notes the phrase is always kept (the timing can't be), and Mixed, the beat menus and the Alphabet's ostinato always keep the timing. This device remembers the choice.
  • Muted lines stay with their bars when bars are added, removed, copied or pasted.
  • You can change the notes while playing the groove and hear what you are doing while editing.
