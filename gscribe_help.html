Help!

Keyboard shortcuts:
  • Spacebar - Start/Stop midi playback
  • Ctrl-Z - Undo
  • Ctrl-Y - Redo
  • Left Arrow - Tempo Down
  • Right Arrow - Tempo Up
  • CTRL and mouse over - turn on notes
  • ALT and mouse over - turn off notes

Saving a groove

You can save a groove by bookmarking the page at any time. You can also "copy and paste", email, or post to social media sites from the share menu.

Metronome

Options:
  • 4ths - adds a cowbell on the 1 and a click on the 2, 3, and 4;
  • 8ths - Adds a click on all the "ands" (plays 8th note triplets in triplet subdivisions)
  • 16ths - Adds a softer click on every 16th note. Plays a louder click on the 2, 3, and 4. (plays 16th note triplets in triplet subdivisions)
  • Solo - Plays the metronome, and mutes the groove when the play button is pressed.
  • Auto Speed Up - Will increase the metronome BPM automatically after each loop through. Tick "Stop at" and enter a tempo to keep increasing until that target tempo, then hold it.
  • Cursor timing - If the blue playback cursor runs ahead of (or behind) the sound on your device, for example over Bluetooth, move the slider until it lines up. Each device remembers its setting.
  • Flams, drags & ruffs - How far apart the grace notes are, and how loud. Spread them right out to practise slowly, then bring them back to a tight 30 ms. The setting is saved in the groove's link, so you can send a student a slowed-down version.
  • Offset click - Allows the metronome to accent the "e", "&", or "a", instead of the 1.

Flams, drags and ruffs

Right click a note on the snare, a tom or a cymbal line (hi-hat, crash or ride) and choose Flam (one grace note), Drag (two) or Ruff (three). The note shows a small label (fl, dr or ru) and the grace notes are written on the sheet music in front of it.
  • Grace notes on… plays and writes the grace notes on a different drum, for example a flam from the snare into the high tom, or a drag on the snare into the floor tom.
  • The grace notes are timed in milliseconds just before their note, whatever the tempo, and never move the notes around them, so you can play exactly what is written at any speed.
  • Use Metronome Options > Flams, drags & ruffs to slow the grace notes down for practice.
  • No flam / drag / ruff takes it off again; turning the note off does too.

What swing is "correct" or "normal"?

There is no "correct" value for swing. The level of swing varies widely and generally left to the performer to determine.
The classic triplet swing feel shows up at 33% on the swing meter. For a tighter swing feel (dotted 8th followed by 16th) move the meter to 50%, full swing. Swing is a matter of personal taste so please feel free to practice your grooves with different amounts of swing until you find the feel that you are looking for.
Wikipedia has a good article on the types of swing. (Scroll down to the "Types" header)

Straight and swung bars

Under each bar's copy and paste buttons there is a small swing switch. Click it to make that bar straight: it then plays straight even though the groove is swung (with the swing slider's amount and style, regular or Brazilian, in the other bars). Click again to swing it. When a swung groove has straight bars, the sheet music shows "Swing" or "Straight" over the first bar and over every bar where the feel changes. The setting is saved in the groove's link.

Brazilian swing

Click the word SWING in front of the swing slider to switch it to BRAZIL, and click again to switch back. Brazilian swing (as in samba and partido alto) works on all four 16th notes of the beat: the "e" is played a little late and the "a" a little early, so the beat feels long-short-short-long: 1 - e & - a. The slider sets how far the "e" and the "a" move. At 10% the beat is split about 27.5% / 22.5% / 22.5% / 27.5%, a typical samba feel; 15% gives about 29% / 21%, and higher settings exaggerate it. In grooves written in 8th notes the same feel is spread over each pair of beats. The setting is saved in the groove's link. The sheet music stays straight, as Brazilian music is normally written.

Editing tips

  • The "Hi-hat", "Snare", "Kick" and "Sticking" labels to the left of the editing grid have a menu when clicked. You can quickly turn all the notes in a measure on or off using the menu.
  • Right click the notes to access special notes, like ghost notes, hi-hat open, flams, drags and ruffs. On a touch device, use the "Advanced Edit" button to access the special notes.
  • Use the Grooves menu to select a groove close to the one you want, or use it to clear the editing grid to start a new groove from scratch.
  • You can change the notes while playing the groove and hear what you are doing while editing.
